## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Expected Solution: Create a digital marketplace that:

• Connects farmers/FPOs directly with consumers and bulk buyers.

• Provides logistics support.

• Uses AI for demand forecasting and route optimization.

Benefits:

• Better prices for farmers.

• Lower prices for consumers.

• Reduced supply chain inefficiencies.

## Proposed Solution

CROPCIRCLE: 

• Demand pooling — households and retailers pre-commit quantity, grade and delivery window.

• Harvest slots — farmers / FPOs list available crops, quantity, harvest date and pickup point

• Smart matching — combine nearby orders and match them to lots through a transparent offer / reverse-auction flow.

• Fair settlement — show farmer net receipt, logistics cost, service fee and buyer price before confirmation.

• Why it is different — clustered demand + harvest timing + shared delivery, with no mandatory middleman.

## Technical Approach

•  Frontend: mobile-first web app; simple farmer / buyer forms. 

•  Backend: Python / Node API, role-based access and order database. 

•  Matching: demand aggregation + lot fit + distance / time scoring. 

•  Logistics: route clustering; pickup and delivery slot planning. 

•  Access: WhatsApp / SMS assisted flow for low-connectivity users. 

•  Build path: prototype → small local pilot → measure price, fill rate and cost/kg.

## Feasibility and Viability

• Feasible MVP — start with one crop cluster, a simple web app and manual dispatch support.

• Demand uncertainty — use pre-orders and minimum order thresholds before confirming a pickup.

• Quality / quantity mismatch — standard lot fields, photo evidence and pickup-side checks.

• Route cost risk — combine nearby drops; compare estimated vs actual cost per kg.

• Digital access — assisted onboarding through FPOs, WhatsApp and SMS.

• Trust & payments — clear terms, digital receipts and documented cancellation rules.

## Impact and Benefits

• Farmers: better visibility of demand; clearer net-price and logistics breakdown.

• Buyers: pooled purchasing, predictable slots and clearer quality / quantity terms.

• Logistics: fewer fragmented trips through clustered pickup and delivery routes.

• Community: stronger farmer–buyer links and a practical route for FPO participation.

• Environment: potential reduction in avoidable transport and unsold produce; validate in pilot.

• Success metrics: farmer net price, buyer price, fill rate, spoilage, cost/kg, payout time.

## Research and References

• e-NAM — National Agriculture Market: https://www.enam.gov.in/ 

• SFAC — Farmer Producer Organisations / market linkage: https://sfacindia.com/ 

• Concept basis — demand aggregation, harvest-slot coordination, shared logistics and transparent settlement.

• Validation needed — pilot interviews with farmers, FPOs, retailers and transporters.

• Pilot data — compare farmer net receipt, buyer price, logistics cost/kg, fill rate and spoilage.

• Status — proposed concept; assumptions and impact must be tested before claiming results.
